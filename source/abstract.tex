Can wider search worsen deployment when the selection score omits a cost? Selection overfitting and the effect of trading costs are known concerns. We use standard finite-pool rank probabilities to distinguish changes in winner exposure from the measured gross-to-net outcome gap. In a trading simulation, we rank four pools of 500--1,500 ridge-regression strategies by gross Sharpe, ignoring execution costs. Wider search selects higher turnover, and the capacity component of the gap, from trade caps and price impact, has a positive fitted slope on the primary search grid in all 16 pool-by-capital cells. From one to 32 trials, mean net Sharpe declines in 11 of the 16 cells. In the other five, including all four pool-A cells, mean net Sharpe rises. The capacity component is not statistically established. Its joint selection--evaluation bootstrap interval for pool B at USD 500 million is $[-0.022,0.121]$. Mean candidate gross Sharpe is negative in every pool. All tested rules except an infeasible rule that selects on evaluation outcomes have negative mean net Sharpe on the comparison grid. Selecting on modeled net Sharpe improves that mean by $0.430$, with paired interval $[0.192,0.723]$, but leaves it at $-0.071$. The calculation makes search-induced exposure to an omitted term explicit. Under the same sampling assumptions, it also applies to validation scores that omit serving cost. It also separates this measurement problem from selection noise. Statistical correction cannot recover a cost the score does not measure.
